# B2 — Plan

## 1. Goal and user-visible result

B2 adds a synthetic, seeded, byte-reproducible commit-message dataset. It is the data B3's eval scores against and B4's classifier is tuned on (dev split only).

When B2 is done:
- `python3 scripts/generate_dataset.py --seed 20261025 --out data/commits.jsonl` writes 360 records.
  - 60 per label, for `feat`, `fix`, `docs`, `refactor`, `test`, `chore`.
  - Standard library only. Templates and vocabulary live in the script.
  - It prints `wrote 360 records to data/commits.jsonl` and exits 0.
- The committed `data/commits.jsonl` is byte-identical to a fresh generation. A test regenerates it and compares.
- Every record follows the roadmap schema: `{"id","message","label","split","slice"}`.
- Splits are exactly 50/50 per label and per (label, slice): 30 dev / 30 test per label.
- Slices per label: `prefixed` 22, `plain` 18, `misleading` 20.
  - Totals: 132 / 108 / 120, that is 36.7 % / 30 % / 33.3 %, each ≥ 20 %.
- The messages are realistic but contain no names, e-mail addresses, URLs, absolute paths, sign-off or co-author footers, and no non-ASCII.
- The `misleading` slice defeats keyword counting by construction. The data stays solvable by a rule-based classifier that reads the leading phrase.
- The test split contains a small amount of held-out vocabulary that never appears in dev. This keeps B4 honest even though the generator is public.
- `check.sh` lints and type-checks `scripts/` as well.

### Generation recipe (what step 1 implements)

**Determinism:**
- One `random.Random(seed)`.
- Use only `rng.randrange(n)`, through a `pick(seq)` helper and a hand-written Fisher–Yates shuffle that also uses `randrange`. No `choice`, `sample`, `shuffle` or `random()`: this keeps the output stable across Python versions.
- Pools are tuples and lists, never sets.
- Labels are iterated in `LABELS` order and slices in `SLICES` order.

**Construction order:**
- For each (label, slice) with count n, produce n records. The first n/2 are `dev`, the rest `test`.
- Reroll a candidate (at most 1000 attempts per record, then raise `RuntimeError`: a generator bug fails loudly) when any of these holds:
  - its summary line, case-folded with whitespace collapsed, already exists anywhere in the dataset (so no dev/test leak through a shared summary);
  - its slice invariant fails;
  - a line exceeds 72 characters.
- After all 360 records exist, shuffle them globally and assign ids `c0001`… in shuffled order, so ids leak neither label nor split.

**Intended signal (what a rule-based classifier can legitimately learn from dev)**
- The label of a `plain` or `misleading` message is decided by its **leading phrase**: the leading verb plus its direct object.
- Unambiguous verbs decide on their own:
  - fix: Prevent, Handle, Avoid, Guard against, Stop, Restore
  - refactor: Extract, Inline, Split, Consolidate, Deduplicate, Rename, Move, Simplify
  - chore: Bump, Pin, Regenerate, Tidy
  - docs: Document, Clarify, Explain, Describe, Reword
  - test: Cover, Parametrize
  - feat: Introduce, Support, Allow, Enable, Expose, Provide
- Ambiguous verbs (Add, Update, Fix, Extend, Write, Upgrade) are decided by the object's head:
  - capability (option, flag, subcommand, setting, mode, output format, hook) → `feat`
  - failure noun (crash, hang, leak, race, off-by-one, wrong exit code, missing check) → `fix`
  - docs artefact (README, docstring, usage example, changelog entry, `docs/<x>.md`, comments) or wording (typo, wording, spelling, grammar) → `docs`
  - test artefact (tests, a test, test cases, a regression test, fixtures, `tests/test_<x>.py`) → `test`
  - tooling artefact (a dependency with a version, CI workflow, lint settings in `pyproject.toml`, pre-commit hooks, Makefile, `.gitignore`, lock file) → `chore`
- Past-tense forms ("Fixed", "Added") appear in about 15 % of summaries and come from shared pools, so dev has them too.
- Bodies (about 30 %) are 1–3 sentences from per-label body pools.
- Each (label, slice) has at least 4 templates. Templates are shared between dev and test.
- Neutral filler is invented and shared: module names such as parser, scheduler, cache, exporter, config loader, report, retry policy, and relative file paths such as `src/<module>.py`.

**Slice invariants** (generator self-check, re-asserted by tests):
- **`prefixed`:**
  - Summary is `<label>[(<scope>)][!]: <summary>`, and the type always equals the label.
  - Scope in about 50 %, from a scope pool; `!` in about 10 %; body in about 30 %; footer in about 20 % (`Refs: #<n>`, `Closes #<n>`, or `BREAKING CHANGE: <text>` only together with `!`).
  - The summary text follows the label's plain recipe, often lower-case after the colon.
- **`plain`:**
  - No Conventional prefix: no line matches `^\s*[A-Za-z]+(\([^)]*\))?!?:\s`.
  - Leading phrase from the label's family.
  - **Zero** hits for any other label's keyword list. Own-label hits are allowed.
- **`misleading`:**
  - No prefix. Leading phrase from the true label's family.
  - Contains decoy material from a decoy label d ≠ label, chosen evenly so that each true label has at least 3 distinct decoys.
  - The keyword count must satisfy `max over other labels of hits > hits(true label)`, strictly.
  - Decoys sit in a tail (`for the crash fix in …`, `used by the release build`, `after the pytest and coverage changes`) or inside a docs-wording or test-artefact leading phrase. They never form another family's leading phrase at the start.
  - Examples:
    - `Add a regression test for the crash fix in the exporter` → test
    - `Fix typo and broken wording in the README install section` → docs
    - `Prevent the release build from hanging on a missing version file` → fix
    - `Introduce a --dry-run flag for the release and build commands` → feat
    - `Extract the error and crash handling into a helper` → refactor
    - `Regenerate the lock file after the pytest and coverage changes` → chore
- **All slices:**
  - ASCII only; no `\r`, tabs or trailing spaces; summary and body lines ≤ 72 characters.
  - None of `@`, `http`, `www.`, `Signed-off-by`, `Co-authored-by`, `/Users/`, `/home/`.

**Held-out vocabulary (B4 honesty, see section 7):**
- `HELDOUT[label]` holds 3–4 entries: object nouns, failure nouns or artefact names. Each entry has at least one distinctive token that appears in no shared pool, template or filler.
- Only `test` records use them. Per label, 4 of the 19 non-prefixed test records do: 2 plain and 2 misleading. That is 24 of 180 test records.
- Of each label's 4, 3 are "secondary": a shared unambiguous verb carries the label and the held-out noun is extra. 1 is "primary": an ambiguous verb plus a held-out artefact noun, a real generalisation probe.
- Worst case if a dev-tuned classifier misses every primary probe: −6/180 = −3.3 % test accuracy.

**Serialisation (byte-identical):**
- Each record is `json.dumps(record, sort_keys=True, ensure_ascii=True)` with default separators.
- Lines are joined with `\n`, the file ends with a final `\n` and is encoded as ASCII.
- It is written to a temp file in the target directory and moved into place with `os.replace`. Parent directories are created.
- If `--out` is an existing directory, the script prints `error: --out is a directory: <path>` to stderr and exits 1.

**Expected control numbers on the test split, by construction (B3 confirms):**
- **majority** (all tie in dev, so `feat` wins): accuracy 0.1667, macro-F1 0.0476, min recall 0.0000, misleading accuracy 0.1667.
- **keyword_spam:**
  - misleading accuracy is 0.0000, because the decoy strictly dominates;
  - per-label recall ≤ 20/30 = 0.6667, so min recall ≤ 0.6667 and accuracy ≤ 0.6667;
  - per-class F1 ≤ 2R/(1+R) ≤ 0.80, so macro-F1 ≤ 0.80.
- **random:** about 0.17 everywhere.
- All of these are below the proposed thresholds (0.85 / 0.85 / 0.70 / 0.70).
- **Classifier ceiling:** prefixed rows are decidable by prefix, and plain and misleading rows by the leading phrase. The only rows a dev-tuned rule set cannot know are the 6 primary probes, so the ceiling is ≥ 0.967 accuracy, ≥ 0.90 misleading accuracy (54/60) and ≥ 0.9667 min recall (29/30).

## 4. Tests (name — what it proves; include error paths and one negative control per metric)

Tests load the generator with `importlib.util.spec_from_file_location`. Its attributes are `cast` to concrete types for `mypy --strict`. Generation in tests runs as a subprocess: `[sys.executable, "scripts/generate_dataset.py", …]`, `cwd` = repo root, `timeout=60`. `pyproject` `pythonpath` is not changed.

**`tests/test_generate_dataset.py`:**
- `test_committed_dataset_matches_fresh_generation`: generate into `tmp_path`; the bytes equal `data/commits.jsonl`.
- `test_byte_identical_across_runs_and_hash_seeds`: runs with `PYTHONHASHSEED=0` and `=1`; the bytes are equal. This proves no set or hash order leaks into the output.
- `test_different_seed_changes_output` (negative control for determinism): seed 1 gives different bytes, so the seed is really used.
- `test_generator_is_stdlib_only`: an AST walk of the imports gives a subset of {`argparse`, `json`, `os`, `re`, `sys`, `random`, `pathlib`, `tempfile`, `dataclasses`, `typing`, `collections`, `itertools`, `__future__`}. No `socket`, `urllib`, `http`.
- `test_seed_not_an_int_is_usage_error` (error path): `--seed abc` gives rc 2 and `usage` on stderr.
- `test_missing_out_is_usage_error` (error path): rc 2.
- `test_out_is_directory_is_error` (error path): `--out tmp_path` gives rc 1 and `is a directory` on stderr; nothing is written.
- `test_keyword_contract`: `CONTROL_KEYWORDS` has exactly the six labels; the lists are pairwise disjoint and equal the literal lists in section 2.
- `test_check_sh_lints_scripts`: a static read of `scripts/check.sh`. The `run_step` ruff-check, ruff-format and mypy lines each contain `src tests scripts`.

**`tests/test_dataset.py`** (reads the committed file):
- `test_file_format`: ends with `\n`, no `\r`, every line is ASCII, and every line equals `json.dumps(json.loads(line), sort_keys=True, ensure_ascii=True)` (canonical form).
- `test_schema`: exactly the five keys, all strings; label in the six; split in {dev, test}; slice in the three; ids match `^c\d{4}$`, are unique and are `c0001`…`c0360` in order.
- `test_counts`: 360 total; 60 per label (≥ 40 holds); per label 22/18/20 by slice.
- `test_split_balance`: per label 30/30; per (label, slice) exactly n/2 each.
- `test_slice_shares`: each slice ≥ 20 % of all records and ≥ 20 % of the test split.
- `test_prefixed_have_matching_prefix`: the summary matches `^(feat|fix|docs|refactor|test|chore)(\([a-z0-9-]+\))?!?: \S` and the type equals the label.
- `test_unprefixed_slices_have_no_prefix`: no line of a plain or misleading message matches the prefix-like pattern, in any case.
- `test_plain_has_no_foreign_keywords`: zero hits for other labels' lists.
- `test_misleading_decoy_dominates`: the fixture that makes keyword_spam fail misleading accuracy and min recall. For every misleading record, max other-label hits > true-label hits. A recomputed keyword_spam (inline, same tokenisation) has test misleading accuracy == 0.0 and per-label test recall ≤ 0.6667.
- `test_majority_bound`: dev is perfectly balanced, so majority accuracy on test is exactly 30/180. This proves the majority control cannot pass.
- `test_no_personal_data` (hard rule 2): no message matches an e-mail regex (`[A-Za-z0-9._%+-]+@[A-Za-z0-9.-]+\.[A-Za-z]{2,}`), a URL regex (`https?://`, `www\.`, `\b[a-z0-9-]+\.(com|org|net|io|dev)\b`), `@`, `Signed-off-by`, `Co-authored-by`, `/Users/` or `/home/`.
- `test_summaries_unique`: summary lines (case-folded, whitespace collapsed) are unique across the whole dataset, so no record is duplicated across dev and test.
- `test_line_lengths`: every line of every message is ≤ 72 characters; there is no trailing whitespace.
- `test_heldout_tokens_only_in_test`: every distinctive token of `HELDOUT` appears in ≥ 1 test record and in **no** dev record. The set H = (test tokens − dev tokens) is non-empty. B4's honesty check depends on this.

Negative control per metric: B2 gates no metric. The tests above prove, per B3 metric, that each control is bounded below threshold on this data:
- majority: `test_majority_bound`;
- keyword_spam: `test_misleading_decoy_dominates`;
- random: by expectation; B3's eval is authoritative.

## 7. Risks and rollback

**Risks:**
- **B4's builder can read the generator.** Options:
  - (a) the "tune on dev only" process rule alone: unenforceable, and the generator shows everything;
  - (b) obfuscated or hashed held-out data: theatre in a transparent public repo;
  - (c) **recommended:** held-out test vocabulary plus a mechanical check plus the process rule.
    - Dev and test share all templates, so dev already reveals everything the generator does, except the held-out vocabulary.
    - The held-out entries are mostly secondary (no advantage from peeking). 6 primary probes measure generalisation.
    - B4 adds a test: no literal or pattern in its rule table contains a token from H = test tokens − dev tokens, computed from `data/commits.jsonl`. That catches peeking mechanically.
    - B4's agent is told not to open `scripts/generate_dataset.py` or test records, and tunes with B3's `eval --split dev [--show-errors]`.
    - Cost: at most −3.3 % test accuracy.
- **Too hard or too easy.** Bounds are by construction (section 2), and step 4 audits them on the real file before review.
- **Python-version drift of `random`.** Only `randrange` with own helpers. The committed-file test catches any drift on the check machine.
- **Hash-order nondeterminism.** No sets; the `PYTHONHASHSEED` test.
- **Keyword-list drift between the generator and `evals/config.json`.** Harmless to safety: B3's eval on the merged tree is authoritative and fails closed if keyword_spam passes. Backlog: B4 may add a consistency test.
- **mypy strict on `importlib`-loaded attributes.** Use `cast` at load time; no `# type: ignore` without a reason.
- **`check.sh` merge with B3.** Disjoint lines with unchanged lines between them; the B3 trial merge proves it.

**Rollback:** all work is on `loop/B2`. Revert a step's commit on the branch; after merge, `git revert -m 1 <merge>` on `main`. B3 must then also wait or revert, because the eval needs the data.
